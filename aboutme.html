About me

I’m an electrical engineering student who has been writing code since I was a kid. I started with Python and have worked in C and C++ ever since. Most of what I build sits close to the hardware or digs into how a system really works.

Outside of class, I design and 3D print parts, solder and rework boards, and self-host a range of services. I also volunteer at BYU-Idaho’s Security Operations Center, which I do for fun and for the chance to see security from the defender’s side.


Where I’m headed

Embedded systems

My goal is to become an embedded systems engineer. I like the constraints of small devices: limited memory, tight timing, and software that has to behave correctly when it touches real hardware.

Wireless and RF

I’ve always been fascinated by wireless telecommunications. Antenna design in particular stands out to me, since it connects the physics of radio waves with the devices I want to build.


Projects

Open source and reverse engineering

Pypush and Beeper

I got involved with Pypush, a cross-platform proof of concept for iMessage, and with the work around Beeper’s iMessage support that grew out of it. I wrote about the experience on my blog.

PyTextNow

I’ve contributed to PyTextNow, a Python library for working with TextNow messaging.

Gabb Phone reverse engineering

I reverse engineered the Gabb Phone and built gabb-adb-gui, a GUI for running ADB commands on the device.

OpenHaystack

I’ve worked with OpenHaystack, the open framework for building custom trackers on Apple’s Find My network.

Embedded

Voxel engine for the TI-84 Plus CE

I wrote wireframe-engine-ce from scratch in C. It renders voxels on a graphing calculator, and I hope to use it for a Temple Run style game one day.
